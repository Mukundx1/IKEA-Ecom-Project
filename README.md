An end-to-end customer analytics project using **SQL, Python, and Power BI** to identify high-value customers, analyze purchasing behavior, and flag potential churn risks using **RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) analysis**.

---

## 📌 Project Overview

IKEA Gifting is a UK-based, non-store online retailer specializing in all-occasion giftware and home décor. Its customer base includes wholesale buyers as well as individual shoppers, making it important to understand customer value beyond individual transaction amounts.

This project analyzes two years of historical transaction data, from December 2009 to December 2011, to develop a structured, data-driven understanding of customer value, purchasing behavior, and retention risk.

The project combines SQL-based data preparation, Python-based customer segmentation, and an interactive Power BI dashboard to translate transaction-level data into actionable business insights.

## 🎯 Business Objectives

The primary objective is to identify the company's most valuable customer segments and quantify churn risk across its customer base.

Key objectives include:

- Identify high-value customers and quantify their contribution to total revenue.
- Detect customers showing signs of disengagement based on purchasing inactivity.
- Analyze customer value and retention trends over time.
- Understand differences in customer value and churn risk across geographic markets.
- Recommend targeted actions for each customer segment.
- Provide an interactive dashboard to support marketing, CRM, retention, and business leadership decisions.

## 🔄 Project Workflow

### 1. Data Extraction and Preparation — SQL

The raw transaction data is processed using SQL to create a consistent, analysis-ready dataset.

Key activities include:

- Combining transaction data across the two years.
- Converting invoice dates into a consistent datetime format.
- Converting unit prices and quantities into appropriate numeric data types.
- Handling missing or invalid values where required.
- Identifying duplicate records and evaluating data quality.
- Preparing a consolidated dataset for downstream analysis.

**Output:** A cleaned and consolidated transaction dataset ready for customer-level analysis.

### 2. Customer-Level Analysis — Python

Python is used to aggregate transaction-level records into customer-level metrics.

The analysis focuses on three key RFM dimensions:

| Metric | Definition | Business Interpretation |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Recency** | Number of days since a customer's most recent purchase | Indicates how recently the customer purchased |
| **Frequency** | Number of distinct purchase transactions during the analysis period | Indicates purchasing consistency and engagement |
| **Monetary** | Total revenue generated by a customer during the analysis period | Measures the customer's historical revenue contribution |

These metrics provide a structured way to compare customers based on their purchasing behavior and historical value.

### 3. RFM Scoring and Customer Segmentation

Customers are assigned RFM scores and classified into meaningful customer segments.

The planned segments are:

| Segment | Description | Potential Business Action |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Champions** | Customers with high RFM scores who represent the most valuable customer group | Prioritize retention, loyalty initiatives, and relationship development |
| **Loyal** | Customers with a consistent purchasing history and moderate to high value | Encourage repeat purchases and strengthen long-term engagement |
| **At Risk** | Previously valuable customers whose purchasing recency has declined | Initiate targeted re-engagement and retention efforts |
| **New** | Recent first-time customers who have not yet established repeat purchasing behavior | Encourage a second purchase and build customer loyalty |
| **Needs Attention** | Customers with relatively low purchasing frequency who may benefit from additional engagement | Evaluate relevant offers and communication strategies |
| **Lost** | Customers with no meaningful purchasing activity over an extended period | Assess reactivation opportunities and prioritize outreach selectively |

> **Note:** The final scoring thresholds and segment assignment rules should be documented alongside the Python implementation.

### 4. Churn Risk Identification

The project uses a fixed **90-day inactivity threshold** to identify customers who may be at risk of churn.

A customer is flagged when no purchase activity is recorded during the trailing 90-day period.

This rule provides a straightforward and interpretable way to identify inactive customers and prioritize retention analysis.

> **Important:** This is a rule-based churn flag, not a predictive machine learning model. It identifies customers meeting the agreed inactivity criterion rather than estimating an individual customer's probability of future churn.

## ⚖️ Scope and Limitations

### In Scope

- SQL-based data extraction and cleaning.
- Python-based RFM calculations and customer segmentation.
- Rule-based churn flagging using a fixed 90-day inactivity threshold.
- Interactive Power BI reporting and drill-down analysis.
- Business insights and segment-level recommendations.

### Out of Scope

- Predictive churn modeling.
- Individual product-level recommendations.
- Real-time or automated customer scoring.
- Execution of marketing or retention campaigns.

### Assumptions and Constraints

- Churn is defined using a fixed 90-day inactivity threshold rather than a statistically derived cutoff.
- Wholesale and individual retail customers are not separated unless the underlying data clearly distinguishes them.
- The analysis is based on historical transaction data and does not explicitly account for external influences such as promotional calendars or seasonality drivers.
